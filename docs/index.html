The toolkit

One local-first companion for the whole search.

Two engines, one repo. JobHelm is the command center that runs the journey — scan, match, apply, connect, prepare, negotiate, land. DeskMock is the rehearsal engine you practice out loud with. Everything is grounded in your real CV, on your own machine and key — and it's a loop: what you learn in each interview feeds straight back into sharper prep.

Prepare is where the learning stack lives. Two feedback loops make it compound: your rehearsal scores drive what you drill next, and each post-interview debrief captures the real questions asked and feeds them back into prep. Every artifact stays grounded in your real CV — the loop makes you sharper, never less truthful.

The rehearsal engine · DeskMock

Rehearse the interview — out loud, on your machine.

Most AI mock-interview tools are cloud SaaS or need a real-time voice stack (LiveKit + cloud speech, often a GPU). DeskMock takes the opposite stance: keep the voice local, keep the brain swappable, keep it a single command. On-device dictation captures your spoken answer and only text reaches the model — on your own key. Point it at a local LLM and nothing leaves your machine at all.

deskmock — mock interview
$ python3 deskmock.py --speak --auto --role "Platform Director" --company "Acme"

🔊 Interviewer ▸ Walk me through the most recent platform you
   standardized for a large org. What were your criteria and the outcome?
   (dictate your answer — DeskMock reads it from the clipboard on Enter)

🎙 You ▸ We consolidated three Kubernetes estates into one paved-road
   platform; measured on DORA metrics and a 30% infra cost cut…

🤖 Feedback ▸ Strong outcome + metrics. Tighten with STAR:
   name the trigger and your specific decision. Power phrase:
   "single paved road, measured by change-fail rate."
QUESTION ▸ How did you drive adoption across resistant teams?

   /done → scorecard  ·  /repeat → hear it again  ·  /skip
macOS say reads questions aloud Local dictation → clipboard Any OpenAI-compatible LLM Per-dimension scorecard Transcripts saved locally Readiness counts real reps

Each rehearsal is scored per dimension and feeds JobHelm's readiness chart — which tells you exactly what to drill next. Prefer to keep hands on keyboard? The same mock runs in the browser inside JobHelm.

Get started

Set up in under a minute.

One clone gives you both engines — JobHelm (the command center) and DeskMock (rehearsal). Pure Python, no dependencies to install. You need Python 3.9+ and an OpenAI-compatible API key (or a local model). Voice is optional; you can start by typing.

1 Get the code (this repo ships both tools)

git clone https://github.com/sundarshankar/deskmock.git cd deskmock

2 Add an LLM key — grab one at openrouter.ai (create a key, add a few dollars of credit; a session costs pennies)

export OPENROUTER_API_KEY=sk-or-your-key-here # prefer a local model? skip the key and pass --base-url http://localhost:11434/v1

3 Run either engine — both work on bundled sample data

# JobHelm — the whole search (opens a demo board at http://localhost:8899) python3 jobhelm/mission-control.py # DeskMock — rehearse out loud (type or dictate; /done for your scorecard) python3 deskmock.py --clipboard

Both run out of the box on the bundled samples, so they work the moment you clone. Point them at your own CV / job search when you're ready — see the docs. For DeskMock voice (read-aloud + hands-free dictation), add --speak.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is this a mock-interview tool or a job-search tool?

Both — it's one toolkit for the whole search. JobHelm runs the pipeline (scan, apply, prepare, negotiate, track); DeskMock is the rehearsal engine you practice out loud with. They ship in one repo and hand off to each other: rehearsal scores drive what you drill, and debriefs feed your next prep.

What stays on my machine?

Your data, your pipeline, your transcripts — there are no servers run by this project and no telemetry. The only thing that leaves is the text sent to the LLM provider you configure (your CV, a JD), on your own key. Point the model at a local endpoint (vLLM / Ollama) and nothing leaves at all. Dictation is on-device; your voice is never uploaded.

Does it work for non-tech roles?

Yes. The prep reads your CV and adapts to your field — a finance candidate gets three-statement modeling, DCF, GAAP, and month-end-close questions; marketing gets analytics and attribution; nursing gets clinical protocols. It only talks Kubernetes if you do. Résumé tailoring, behavioral prep, the story bank, and negotiation are field-agnostic by design.

Which LLMs work? Can I run it free?

Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. The default is OpenRouter with deepseek/deepseek-v3.2 (pennies per session). Or run it free on Google's Gemini free tier: set JOBHELM_BASE_URL to Gemini's OpenAI-compatible endpoint, JOBHELM_API_KEY to a free AI Studio key, and JOBHELM_MODEL=gemini-2.5-flash. Local models (Ollama/vLLM) work too.

What operating system do I need?

macOS, Windows, or Linux. The whole search — board, prep, résumé tailoring, scanning, and in-browser rehearsal — is cross-platform (files open with your OS default app on each). The only macOS-only extras are DeskMock's read-aloud voice (--speak) and hands-free dictation (--auto); on Windows/Linux those step aside gracefully and you type your answers.

Is it really free?

Yes — open source under the MIT license. You only pay for your own LLM usage (or nothing, if you run a local model).

Can I trust what it generates?

Treat it as a strong assistant, not an oracle. It's AI-assisted and grounded in your real CV with accuracy checks, but outputs can still be wrong — review and verify everything, and you're responsible for the truthfulness of your résumé.
